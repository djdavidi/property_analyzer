## What the money means

- **Current charge:** computed from the recorded billing areas when this page was made; not yet compared with the published charge or an actual invoice.
- **Example saving:** what a credit could be worth if the missing checks pass. It is not a forecast or approved credit.
- **Cannot estimate yet:** we need more information. It does not mean $0.
- **Do not add the examples:** some may cover the same ground or represent different assumptions.

## What to take away

**Roof and pavement area on the bill:** we have not mapped enough of the ground material to prove how much of the billed area is wrong. The difference between the bill and the partial roof map is not an appeal amount.

**Trees:** the images show tree crowns, but not whether pavement lies beneath them. The report shows an example credit only if enough qualifying pavement is present. For Springside, the example is up to about $13,617/year across its three billing parcels. That is not a confirmed or expected saving.

**Permeable pavement:** mapped candidates at City Ave, Springside and Delaware Ave deserve a closer look. Check approved pavement plans, current condition and existing credits. Separate examples may overlap, so they are not added into a site total.

**Rain gardens, basins and other systems:** a mapped system does not prove where runoff enters or whether the system treats enough water. Start with the approved drainage and treatment plan.

**Other credits and refunds:** green roofs, drainage onto planted ground, open space, waterway discharge, industrial permits and past billing issues are all included in the full report. No final amount has been established for these. The report explains what is missing for each one.

## Where the missing information may come from

Public plans and permit records may answer some questions. Actual bills, credit approval letters, maintenance records and site checks may be needed for the rest. This wording update did not fetch new information or rerun the properties.
